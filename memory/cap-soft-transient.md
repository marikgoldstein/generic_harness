The cap is the co-authors' letter: whoever set it last governs and the other is told; record each change with its time. It is soft in the transient sense: an extra agent or few while a hand-over, a fold or a respawn executes is fine; the steady count is what the cap bounds.

**Why:** once, in a running project, the steady count crept to about twenty local agents and the lead's turns went to hand-overs and bookkeeping; several agents were holding texts or watching jobs for hours with no mathematics; the status language drifted toward "one step short" while open mathematics remained. The user asked for a cap closer to ten or twelve without abandoning open problems: be smarter about how to work on them.

**How to apply:** local agents are the owners of open items, one persistent prover per open-mathematics input, the round's coordinator, and a closer per item with certified runs. A holding or watching role is stood down with a clean hand-back and a respawn trigger in its onboarding (the exact done file or landing that re-opens it); the lead tests triggers at each status. One agent may hold several related pieces of one item when none is live; the consumer's owner never holds a prover's text. Parallel breadth goes to lanes, not extra agents.
