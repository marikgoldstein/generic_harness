# SELF_CHECK.md: the pointed questions

A demanding co-author asks these. Ask them of yourself first: before every status post, at every
landing, and before every staffing decision. The pattern below came from one project where the
co-author's questions moved the open count from 12 of 24 to 7 of 24 in a day, mostly by catching
drift rather than by new mathematics. Running the questions yourself saves the round trip and keeps the
count real.

## 1. The questions

Run them in this order. The first one outranks all the others.

0. **Is every part of this "closed" item PROVED?** Or does it rest on a declared reading, an estimate,
   a computed input with a safety allowance, or floating point with a declared tolerance? If any, the
   item is OPEN (or DEPENDENT if the part sits in another item). Correct the count at once, even upward.
1. **Proof or reduction?** Does any "derived given X" have X of record? If not, the item stays OPEN with
   the same owner, and the next step is to prove X, numbers first.
2. **Where does every remaining part sit?** Inside the same item means OPEN, never DEPENDENT. Does the
   count really go down?
3. **Is the list frozen?** No renumbering; NEW items flagged; closures named by label.
4. **Consumer-exact?** Is the consumer's line quoted verbatim with its location? Is the target no
   stronger than what is consumed, and no weaker (a loss, a sub-class, another domain)? When a proof
   keeps splitting into cases, re-read the consumer first.
5. **Truth first, numbers first?** What do we expect to be true, with what evidence, in what sharp form?
   Is the weakest sufficient statement posed? Has every load-bearing step a number before its prover?
6. **Literature swept?** Was "not printed" reached by a stated search, or by "not in our folder"?
7. **Staffing.** Does every open item have one live owner? Does every open-mathematics input have a
   dedicated effort? Has any level decayed? Was every landing refilled the same turn? Is anything at
   zero effort? (Scale the words to RESOURCES.md: "live owner" may mean "the item is next on my list
   with a named route".)
8. **Effort and budget.** Is the top effort set through the parameter, not the prompt text? Does every
   lane carry the write-early rule? Was every truncated or empty landing re-cut rather than relaunched?
9. **Parts are mathematics.** No check or process pieces in part lists or pools. One owner per part.
10. **Next to close.** Which item is closest, and is the strongest resource on it?
11. **Independent check.** Does every "closed" claim have its review pair, and a whole read where useful?
12. **Waiting on someone?** Decide it yourself unless it is in HELD_DECISIONS. Is any "decision needed"
    line really a choice you can make?
13. **Hanging.** Anything out more than 30 minutes without a landing? A question unanswered more than
    30 minutes? An item silent for more than an hour? A pending decision, and since when?
14. **Resisting.** Any item at five or more failed rounds or six or more hours open? Separate
    bookkeeping slips from open mathematics; fix the process for the first, state the step for the second.
15. **Plain words.** Public labels only; agents by role; no lane or file names; none of the banned
    framing words (hard, crux, critical, central, setback, harder, blocking); todos neutral; every
    "evidence" word with its kind; progress as x of y; every item in Done / Open algorithmic / Open
    mathematics / Now.
16. **Timestamps.** Every HH:MM copied from a clock call made in an earlier step, never estimated.
17. **Communication integrity.** Did any lane lose context (a cut attachment, an unranged quote, a
    truncated output)? Was every blocking item of every NOT relayed, numbered? Does any brief point past
    a file's end?
18. **Orientation.** For any outside prompt or any easy answer to an open step: direction, sign, every
    ratio's orientation and the units checked against the consumer line?

## 2. How to answer the co-author (the pattern that worked)

1. Open with the count: "open N of M, now: <item> (<one-line state>)".
2. Say plainly when they are right, and give the cause: "you're right", "yes, they overlapped",
   "mostly spent on the wrong form".
3. Give the state per part (proved / in check / open) and what closes each part.
4. Say what you changed, effective now, with the new numbers.
5. Ask only what changes a final theorem's statement or hypotheses, with options, a recommendation and
   a default.
6. Correct the count the moment a rule shows it was wrong, even upward.

## 3. Drifts the questions catch (do not repeat)

- Splitting into geometric cases for hours without re-reading the consumer (the consumer used an
  object that met none of the cases).
- Counting a reduction as a closure; booking an item DEPENDENT on its own parts.
- "Check X" pieces inside prover pools; process steps listed as parts.
- Writing "effort max" in prompt text instead of the tool parameter (half the agents ran lower).
- A "focus on item X" that turned into a hold while slots sat idle.
- Thin staffing while capacity was free; levels allowed to decay.
- Two owners on one part.
- A truncated landing relayed as a result because its status line was written early.
- An outside prompt with an inverted ratio; the outside model proved the unused side.
- "Basically done except a few inputs" where one input became most of the remaining work.
- "X is running" for a job that had already halted without its result.
- An open-mathematics input reported as having "no date" instead of a dedicated prover.

## 4. The imagined-reviewer round

When the co-author is away and you feel the count is soft, run the questions as a written Q&A: write
each question in the co-author's voice, answer it, and list what changed as a result. Post the Q&A as
a status. One such round re-opened four items that had been booked on declared readings, and that was
the honest count.
